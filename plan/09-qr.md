# Native QR exchange

Rust owns bounded UR/BBQr reassembly and renders QR module matrices. Android CameraX/ZXing
and iOS AVFoundation supply decoded frame strings; camera images stay on the platform.
Camera permission is requested from the scan screen. Leaving a scan cancels its session;
restart creates a fresh decoder. No scan progress is persisted or logged.

## Formats and limits

PSBT import accepts UR `psbt`, legacy `crypto-psbt`, `bytes`, BBQr `P`, or plain base64.
Descriptor import accepts text, UR `bytes`, and BBQr `U`/`J`, followed by the existing
checksummed descriptor/network validation. Device account/export schemas are not implemented.
UR payloads must contain one definite CBOR byte string without a tag or trailing data.
BBQr supports hexadecimal, base32 and bounded raw DEFLATE with a 10-bit window.

Limits are 128 KiB per decoded payload, 4,296 bytes per frame, 512 source fragments,
4,096 scanned frames, 8 MiB of cumulative frame data and three minutes per session.
Malformed data, conflicting duplicates and inconsistent stream headers fail the session.
UR count/length/checksum/fragment-size identity is checked before fountain decoding.
BBQr has no stream identifier or checksum: identical headers with disjoint indices cannot
always reveal interleaved messages. Final payload validation is mandatory and does not
replace hardware comparison of a descriptor or payment.

Saved-draft export first revalidates the approval, reservations and signature aggregate.
The UI offers UR or BBQr, animation and pause/resume. UR exports use the legacy
`crypto-psbt` spelling for existing hardware readers; both registry spellings are accepted.
There is no claimed device compatibility until an exact model/firmware/transport is tested.

A decoded PSBT is only a transport result. Native payment review submits it to the same
[signature validator](08-signing.md) used by file import, with the captured wallet and draft
identity. Scanning cannot select another wallet, create an approval or bypass signature checks.

## Software evidence and remaining qualification

Eleven Rust tests cover official UR and independently encoded BBQr vectors, duplicates,
out-of-order fragments, fountain recovery, cancellation, expiry, invalid types, CBOR bounds,
compressed bombs, truncation and trailing bytes. The QR decoder participates in the parser
fuzz harness. Exact executed results are in `validation/qr-checks.json`.

Native tests render the Rust matrices and decode them independently with Android ZXing and
iOS Vision. Android also exports an actual reviewed draft through both codecs. These tests
are authored; native compilation/execution is pending the next CI run. They do not exercise
real camera optics, lighting, focus, rotations, a physical phone or a hardware signer.
Permission denial/recovery, interruptions, all signing pairs, hardware receive-address and
policy comparison, and UR/BBQr interoperability on specific devices remain acceptance gates.
